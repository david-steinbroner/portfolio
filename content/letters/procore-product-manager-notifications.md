From my first months at Fold, a bitcoin rewards fintech, I kept hearing the same complaint: users had no meaningful control over what we sent them. I believed a [notification preference center](/case-studies/notification-preference-center) was table stakes, but it stayed unprioritized for nearly three years. When engineering still had no bandwidth, I took it from vendor selection and product design through implementation, launch, training, and iteration.

50% of daily active users adopted it in the first month. Giving users control over email, push, and in-app notifications lifted per-channel engagement about 10% month over month. The preference and engagement data then became an input into later product rollouts.

Fold was a bitcoin rewards fintech when I joined as employee 11. I stayed more than four years as it grew to around 40 people, evolved to a complete financial platform, and became the first bitcoin-only company listed on the Nasdaq. Important work moved when someone was willing to carry it across functions.

The preference center sat on top of Iterable, a lifecycle messaging platform I had led Fold onto when I joined. I migrated email off Mailchimp, established push and in-app messaging, connected the user database, and built the templates, journeys, segments, and automations supporting communications across more than 500,000 users. I chose PushPlanet, a notification-preferences vendor, for the preference layer because it integrated with Iterable and could extend across all three channels.

That sequence is why this role feels familiar. Procore needs to migrate product teams onto a shared platform, reduce communication fatigue, and use engagement data to decide what to fix next, while powering more than one billion annual emails alongside real-time in-app alerts. A customer on your own community forum put it plainly: "FAR TOO MANY emails," and called it the number one complaint of their subcontractors.

I have also done the internal alignment work. At Fold, I moved several stakeholder groups onto one shared [banking-partner approval process](/case-studies/banking-partner-approval), cutting approval time about 75%, from four-plus weeks to days.

I now build and ship live products with Claude Code through feature branches, pull requests, CI checks, Sentry, and PostHog. They are live at davidsteinbroner.com.

I am in Austin, available immediately, and ready to work on-site downtown. Hand me the next team that needs to migrate onto Notification Platform, and I will start by mapping what it sends today, where the workflow breaks, and what needs to change for the migration to stick.
