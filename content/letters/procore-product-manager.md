At Mosaic, a solar finance company, I started by reading support tickets one by one. The same problems kept showing up, but nobody was tracking them. I mapped the patterns, wrote the company's first process documentation, and rebuilt the installer onboarding flow in Salesforce while sitting between the account managers in the field and the rest of the business. Installer-side support tickets fell about 50%. Installer onboarding velocity doubled month over month.

I carried that into enterprise B2B onboarding at Sensor Tower, a digital market insights and analytics platform, for clients including Calm and Headspace, and fed what I saw in their adoption back into product priorities. Then I joined Fold as employee 11, spent four years there as it went from startup to the first bitcoin-only listing on the Nasdaq, and moved into product with limited design and engineering bandwidth.

My strongest tripod project was a [tax-compliance integration](/case-studies/taxbit) at Fold. I ran it with 1 designer, 2 engineers, 6 stakeholder groups with veto power, and an external vendor. I picked the vendor, negotiated the contract, drew the wireframes, wrote the user documentation, and trained support. We shipped 2.5 months early, reached 90% user adoption, and held the support-ticket rate to 2%.

I also built and ran a 40+ person beta group that tested every major feature before launch. Their feedback sat alongside signals from app stores, Twitter, Discord, email, and support, then went into a ranked product view the team could act on. That's the kind of beta-to-GA work I want to own for Time Tracking and Equipment.

Construction is new to me. The working pattern is familiar. Mosaic taught me how to operate inside a regulated, multi-party, field-facing business where the field sees the friction first. I'd bring that same daily customer cadence to Procore, then pair it with the shipping discipline I built at Fold.

I did some research and saw that Procore bought Datagrid in January and shipped the Daily Log and Contract Review agents in May with a person approving every action. I build my own multi-agent tooling on that same model. The agents draft, I hold the gate, nothing goes out unread. Those products are live at davidsteinbroner.com.

I'm in Austin. Hand me the beta list, and I'll start by talking to customers, ranking what's blocking adoption, and helping the team move the right fixes into GA.
